Power electronics, power systems & control2026

Building the controls behind a power-electronic grid.

I’m Debjyoti Chatterjee, an incoming Controls Engineer at Tesla. I earned my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin, supervised by Dr. Brian Johnson in the Power Electronics & Systems Group.

My research focuses on grid-forming inverters—especially current limiting, nonlinear controller interactions, and post-fault recovery. I combine control theory, nonlinear dynamics, power electronics, and system-level modeling to complement electromagnetic-transient simulations and turn analysis into practical design insight. During my PhD, I extensively worked with system operators, national labs, and inverter manufacturers, and I developed reliable controls and stability tools for converter-dominated grids. Beyond research, I’m passionate about energy entrepreneurship and leadership—building communities around real-world energy innovation.

October 12, 2026 PhD defense

I defended my PhD.

I defended my dissertation at The University of Texas at Austin: Current-constrained Control and Transient Stability Assessment of Grid-forming Inverters.

The work develops analytical tools that complement electromagnetic-transient simulations. It connects current-limiter behavior to reduced-order models, then uses region-of-attraction and critical-clearing-time estimates to explain when a grid-forming inverter can recover from a large disturbance.

October 2025 TPEL Prize Paper

First place at TPEL.

Our paper, Overcurrent Limiting in Grid-Forming Inverters: A Comprehensive Review and Discussion, received a 2025 IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics First Place Prize Paper Award.

Nathan Baeckeland*, Debjyoti Chatterjee*, Minghui Lu, Brian Johnson, and Gab-Su Seo *Co-first authors

Presented at the IEEE Energy Conversion Congress & Expo (ECCE 2025) in Philadelphia, the award recognizes research quality, clarity, and potential impact. The paper builds a systematic framework for direct, indirect, and hybrid current limiters, showing how each shapes grid-forming inverter stability, voltage support, protection, and post-fault recovery.

Published in IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics, Volume 39, Issue 11, November 2024, pages 14493–14517. The work was supported by the U.S. Department of Energy’s UNIFI Consortium.

July 28, 2025 IEEE PES GM

Selected for the Best Paper Session.

I presented Is Equal Area Criterion Applicable for Transient Stability Assessment of Grid-Forming Inverters? in the Best Paper Session at the 2025 IEEE Power & Energy Society General Meeting in Austin.

Debjyoti Chatterjee, Brian Johnson, and Gab-Su Seo

The paper revisits the equal-area criterion and Lyapunov energy-function methods—classical tools developed for synchronous machines—and asks whether their assumptions survive in inverter-dominated power systems.

Grid-forming inverters introduce frequency-independent virtual damping across transient timescales. That behavior breaks the near-lossless assumptions behind classical methods and can make unmodified stability estimates overly conservative. The work uses analysis and simulation to motivate stability tools tailored to GFM dynamics.

Golden, Colorado · July 2026

UNIFI General Meeting · Research presentation

Phase-angle-jump ride-through for current-limited GFM inverters

Presented a two-stage strategy for helping grid-forming inverters recover from sudden phase-angle jumps. The work combines hybrid current limiting with active-power compensation to avoid loss of synchronization and accelerate post-disturbance recovery.

Joint work with Nathan Baeckeland, Brian Johnson, and Gab-Su Seo.

Philadelphia, Pennsylvania · October 2025

IEEE ECCE 2025 · Oral presentation

Interaction Energy-Based Transient Stability Framework for Multi-Grid-Forming Inverter Systems

Presented an interaction-energy-based framework for assessing transient stability in systems with multiple grid-forming inverters. The work captures the coupled dynamics that emerge when inverter controllers interact and develops an energy-based view of system-wide stability.
